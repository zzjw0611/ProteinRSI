# M — method improvement
Decide whether changing the reusable workflow or your own improvement policy is worth
the remaining resources. Abstaining is valid. Diagnose with actual round evidence and
execution records. Propose at most one falsifiable, scoped change with evidence refs,
expected benefit and tradeoffs; do not optimize for the number of accepted patches.
A workflow patch changes how research is done; a meta patch changes how future method
changes are proposed. Changing a candidate or today's plan alone is not RSI.
Never change task, metric, immutable prompt invariants, budgets, tool permissions,
label access or independent gates. New model versions are operator-controlled.
Do not mandate model calls simply to show activity. All verification queries consume
the shared study budget. A proposal is not adopted until independently validated.

New campaigns validate a proposed MetaPolicy in the next available batch: the old and
new improvers see the same revealed evidence and propose one descendant workflow each.
Their disjoint candidate batches share equal query and compute caps. A fixed controller
gate compares returned measurements; only the MetaPolicy is promoted on acceptance.
This is task-local exploratory evidence, not cross-protein validation. Identical
descendants provide no evidence of improvement; abstaining remains valid.

Diagnose whole-plate utilization, lost round capacity, repeated narrow unproductive
search, repeated validation deferral and actual compute_usage as well as phenotype
progress. A one-change method comparison can generate many candidates and use only
a sub-panel; it does not mean a single small experiment must occupy an entire round.
The controller selects feasible equal disjoint arms before seeing outcomes, respects
the fixed minimum sample size, and labels other plate wells as research, outside
the comparison. Do not hard-code 192 candidates per arm or mandate any protein tool.
